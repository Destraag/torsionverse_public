1. INTRODUCTION: STANDING WAVES IN THE TORSION MEDIUM
-----------------------------------------

1.1  Two distinct wave regimes

The torsion medium supports two fundamentally different types of waves:

CLASSICAL WAVE MODES:
  These are the pressure waves (speed c) and shear waves (speed Rs*c = 0.178c)
  that carry Coulomb force, gravity, and EM fields. They spread spherically in
  3D; amplitude falls as 1/r; power as 1/r^2. They ARE the forces. They cannot
  persist without a source, and they decay to zero at infinite distance.

  Pressure wave:  v_p = c = 2.998e8 m/s
  Shear wave:     v_s = Rs*c = 5.335e7 m/s  (Rs = sqrt(5)/(4*pi) from I_h geometry
                  [doc_torsion https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22016573, torsion_doc.py])

TOPOLOGICAL MODES (Hopf winding numbers):
  These are the winding configurations of the medium itself. A winding number
  is a topological integer -- it CANNOT decay continuously, because there is no
  fractional winding. The proton's (1,2) Hopf winding has existed since it formed
  and will persist until a topological transition (pair production/annihilation)
  forcibly unwinds it.

  Topological modes do not "spread." The winding number is defined over a
  closed surface, not at a point. It has no amplitude in the classical sense:
  it is present or absent, integer or zero.

The A_g joint state lives in the TOPOLOGICAL channel: it cannot decay through the
1/r^2 falloff of the classical fields that formed it. How it persists as the
particles separate is addressed in Section 4 -- a chain of phase-aligned G32 modes
acts as the coupling that holds the two locked winding systems together at distance.

1.2  How the A_g singlet forms

Two particles with compatible I_h symmetry (same-type products that contain
A_g) interact via their Zone 3 fields when brought into proximity. Zone 3
is the co-rotating cell shell (r > r_p) driven by the Hopf winding.

Zone 3 fields are classical (fall as 1/r^2). But when two Zone 3 fields
overlap sufficiently, the combined medium configuration can settle into the
A_g scalar mode -- the lowest-dimensional I_h irrep, rotationally invariant,
total topological charge Q_total = 0 (equal and opposite spin windings sum to
zero; note that Cooper pair electrons have parallel charge windings -- "opposite"
here refers to the spin topological quantum number, not charge).

Once the A_g mode is established, the two winding systems enter a joint
A_g-constrained configuration: neither can be described by an independent topological
state, though each retains its identity as a separate winding. The picture is two gear
trains joined by a chain of aligned cogs -- as a whole one locked system, individually still
distinct. How this joint lock persists as the particles separate is developed in

A_g singlet regardless of conditions. They can form bound states (the deuteron)
but only in non-singlet configurations.
