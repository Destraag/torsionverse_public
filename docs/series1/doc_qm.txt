4.1  Why interference occurs

The proton (or electron) is a corpuscle -- always at one definite location,
never split or delocalized. Its Zone 3 field extends to infinity (falling

When the corpuscle approaches a barrier with two slits:

  (a) The CORPUSCLE goes through exactly ONE slit -- always definite, never
      "at slit A and slit B simultaneously." What spans both slits is its
      Zone 3 pressure field, which reaches both openings no matter which
      slit the corpuscle itself takes (fields fall off with distance but
      extend everywhere; they are not confined to one opening).
  (b) The medium pressure field passes through BOTH slit openings
      simultaneously, whichever slit the corpuscle used.
  (c) At the screen, the wave contributions from both slits
      superpose: constructive where path difference = n*lambda_dB,
      destructive otherwise. This superposition is a property of the FIELD;
      it is not a claim that the corpuscle itself was in two places.
  (d) Interference pattern = standard Huygens-Fresnel diffraction for the medium
      wave. Fringe spacing = lambda_dB / d where d is slit separation.
      lambda_dB = 2*pi*hbar / (m*v) = de Broglie wavelength (from KG dispersion).

Photons (theta=0, no Hopf winding) have no Zone 3 shell -- a Zone 3 shell
is structurally tied to having a winding at all. The field that spans both

the fringe-spacing formula never depend on the field being scalar (A_g)
specifically, only on two coherent contributions sharing a common
oscillating axis -- automatic for a scalar field, and equally true for a
vector (T_1g) field whenever both slit-paths carry the same polarization
(the default case for a simple, single-source setup). lambda_dB for a
photon uses its momentum p = E/c directly (lambda_dB = 2*pi*hbar/p), the
same relation specialized to m=0 rather than a different formula.

The corpuscle's subsequent path to the screen is drawn from the resulting

This is also why a single detection event is always a single, localized

the screen.

  - "Measure now" vs. "measure later, before the field reaches the screen":

No retrocausality, and nothing exotic required: the corpuscle's path was
already a settled fact before either choice was made. The only thing
"delayed" is when a real, local, sub-c perturbation reaches the
still-traveling field -- a question about signal propagation, not about

5.2  Quantum erasure: the general class

A quantum eraser experiment first makes which-path information available
(destroying interference, Section 4.2), then erases it before detection,
recovering interference in a conditioned sub-ensemble. Two structurally
different cases exist, both covered by mechanisms already established
above -- no additional postulate is needed to distinguish them.

SINGLE-CORPUSCLE ERASERS (no entanglement): one corpuscle, one detection
event. There is no separate, always-fires-regardless detector analogous
to D0 below -- the corpuscle's own click IS the whole trial. Which-path
marking and its erasure both act on the SAME corpuscle's Zone 3 field
(Section 4.2/4.2a); fringes appear directly in the raw, unconditioned
data whenever the marking is erased before detection, with no coincidence
counting required. Section 4.2a's beamsplitter-eraser example is of this
type.

TWO-CORPUSCLE (ENTANGLED-PAIR) ERASERS: a single initial corpuscle is
converted into a correlated pair (e.g. by SPDC, Section 5.3), one member
going to a position-sensitive detector (D0) that fires on EVERY trial
regardless of the partner's fate, the other carrying which-path
information to a separate measurement. Because D0's raw data mixes both
marked and erased trials together, it never shows fringes unconditioned,
with or without erasure (Section 5.3) -- recovering fringes requires
sorting D0's already-recorded clicks using a SECOND, independent
measurement on the partner corpuscle.

Is that second requirement a deep new principle, or just bookkeeping?
Both, partly: there is no exotic physics in "two separate particles need
two separate measurements" -- but the STRUCTURAL FEATURE this creates (an
independent, always-firing detector whose data only reveals pattern
structure once conditioned on a second measurement) is real, and is what
Section 5.3 works through for one specific experiment. Whether the same
A_g-singlet/G32-chain correlation mechanism used elsewhere in this
framework (doc_entanglement.txt) cleanly covers the photon pairs used by
most tabletop erasers is a separate, open question -- docs/open_items.txt
item 17.

5.3  Kim et al. 2000: a worked two-corpuscle example

Kim et al.'s setup uses spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC): just
past the slits, a nonlinear crystal converts the corpuscle that crossed the
double slit into an entangled PAIR -- a "signal" corpuscle continuing to a
screen detector D0, and an "idler" corpuscle sent along a longer, separate
path to one of four detectors (D1-D4).

Momentum conservation in the down-conversion fixes the idler's propagation
direction to depend on which slit the original corpuscle used -- ordinary
conservation applied to the splitting process, not a new torsionverse
postulate (see NOT YET DERIVED, below). Downstream, the idler path is
either:
  - kept separate (D3/D4): which slit is still recoverable from the idler
    alone -> the D0 clicks paired with these show NO interference.
  - recombined at a beamsplitter (D1/D2): the two slit-correlated idler
    paths become indistinguishable before detection -> which-slit
    information is erased -> the D0 clicks paired with these DO show
    interference. This recombination is the same Huygens-superposition
    mechanism as Section 4.1's own double slit, just run on the idler's
    two paths instead of the signal's.

THE CENTRAL FACT, confirmed by the real experiment (not a torsionverse-
specific claim): D0's own pattern, summed over ALL idler outcomes with no
sorting, never shows interference -- with or without "erasure." Fringes
appear only in the sub-ensemble of D0 clicks whose entangled idler partner
later lands in D1 or D2; the D3/D4-paired sub-ensemble shows plain two-slit
diffraction. This is a statement about which already-recorded clicks get
grouped together, not about any click's own pattern changing in real time.

Why this needs no retrocausality (a two-corpuscle correlation, stronger
than Section 5.1's single-corpuscle argument): signal and idler are
correlated at the moment they are created, via an A_g-locked joint
configuration (doc_entanglement.txt Section 1.2; the optical-photon case
is an easy, low-threshold instance of the same mechanism, doc_entanglement.
txt Section 4.3(a)). By doc_entanglement.txt's independently closed no-FTL
result (Section 4.4, EQ8), resolving that correlation -- whichever
corpuscle is measured first -- cannot propagate information faster than c.
Sorting D0's clicks by a later idler measurement does not change D0's own
already-registered data; it only reveals, after the fact, which
sub-ensemble a given click belongs to.
[Consistent with quantum eraser experiments by Kim et al. 2000]

NOT YET DERIVED: the geometric mapping from "which slit" to "which idler
propagation direction" is imported here from standard SPDC momentum
conservation. Whether the torsion medium's own picture of down-conversion
(a corpuscle splitting into two lower-energy corpuscles at a nonlinear
crystal) reproduces this mapping from Jobson-cell mechanics, rather than
importing it from standard nonlinear optics, is an open question -- see
Section 6.

5.3.1  Creation-nexus rule: narrowing the SPDC-mapping gap (2026-10-05)

A specific hypothesis proposes a definite answer to the "NOT YET DERIVED"
question just above: suppose SPDC couples to the FIELD at the crystal
(which, per Section 4.1, already spans both slits there regardless of
which slit the real corpuscle took) rather than to the corpuscle's
already-committed slit. If the down-conversion's signal+idler phases SUM-
lock to the pump (ordinary energy/momentum conservation) while their
DIFFERENCE stays free to carry the slit-A-vs-slit-B information, the
signal's own conditional field amplitude at D0 (given which idler path)
inherits the same x-dependent relative phase an ordinary double slit
would produce.

Tested in analysis/quantum/kim_eraser_creation_nexus_test.py (10/10 checks
PASS): routed through the real apparatus (BSa/BSb's direct ports to
D3/D4, reflected portions recombined at the shared beamsplitter BSc
using ordinary 50/50 unitarity -- the same +/- sign transformation the
original paper itself cites, not an added assumption), this reproduces
the paper's own reported structure
exactly: R01(x)=env(x)*cos^2(pi*d*x/(lambda*f)), R02(x)=env(x)*sin^2(pi*d*x/
(lambda*f)), R03(x)=R04(x)=env(x)/2 (matched single-peak envelopes, no
fringes) -- using only machinery already established elsewhere in this
document (Section 4.1's Huygens/field mechanism, applied a second time)
plus standard beamsplitter unitarity. No G32 chain, no winding, no new
ontology.

What this does and does not close: the phase-sum-lock rule ITSELF remains
imported from standard SPDC conservation -- this result narrows, but does
not fully close, the gap above (it shows the observed pattern follows
automatically once that one rule is granted; it does not derive the
down-conversion process itself from Jobson-cell mechanics). Status:
exploratory, under review -- see docs/open_items.txt item 16.

5.3.2  Proposed controls to isolate entanglement's role (2026-10-10)

Three controls address different confounds. C1 and C3 rule out
apparatus-level confounds that are NOT specific to entanglement --
beamsplitter-count artifacts, detector-hardware/position effects -- and
so are theory-agnostic: their predictions test whether the observed
pattern genuinely tracks the recombination/erasure FUNCTION, not some
other apparatus detail that could ALSO happen to involve entanglement
but isn't limited to it. C2 is the only control that directly
manipulates entanglement itself, and so is the only one that can
separate "genuine entanglement" from "mere classical correlation" as
the fringe pattern's actual cause. None of the three have been built;
each has a distinguishable, falsifiable prediction.

C1 -- SYMMETRIC NON-RECOMBINING CONTROL (tests whether mere apparatus
complexity -- beamsplitter count -- rather than actual recombination of
path information, is what produces fringes). Two ALTERNATIVE approaches
to this same question, each with a different tradeoff (not
complementary pieces of one combined control -- pick one):
  C1a: reroute the actual erasure-bound beams (Ma's and Mb's reflected
    outputs) onto two separate, non-mixing beamsplitters (BSc fed by
    slit A alone; a new BSd fed by slit B alone -> new detectors D5,
    D6) instead of the shared BSc -- removing ALL erasure/recombination
    while keeping the same winding/path structure and beamsplitter
    count. TRADEOFF: this also removes the recombining phase itself --
    which is part of the complexity being tested -- along with
    everything else, so a null result here does not cleanly isolate
    "beamsplitter count alone" from "no recombination at all."
  C1b: achieves the same underlying test without that tradeoff -- add
    a second beamsplitter BSd in front of the EXISTING D4 only,
    relabeling its two outputs D4/D5, leaving D1/D2's real erasure
    process completely untouched. D4/D5 now pass through 2
    beamsplitters total before detection (matching the erasure path's
    count) with NO merging of path information at any stage (BSd has
    only one input), while D1/D2 continue to show the genuine,
    unmodified recombination exactly as in the real experiment -- a
    cleaner, non-invasive isolation of the same question C1a targets.
  EXPECTED outcome under ordinary optics, NOT a settled prediction:
  since neither variant ever coherently recombines light from both
  slit-paths at a single detector, textbook diffraction theory says no
  fringes should appear in the relevant control channel(s). But this is
  exactly what the control needs to establish empirically, not assume
  -- it is entirely possible that apparatus complexity alone (sheer
  beamsplitter count, imperfections in the optical elements, etc.) DOES
  produce fringe-like structure despite no genuine recombination, which
  is precisely why this test is worth running rather than dismissed as
  foregone. A positive result (fringes appearing anyway) would be the
  important, diagnostic finding -- evidence that something other than
  genuine erasure/recombination is responsible for the original
  pattern. No literature precedent found for either exact variant
  (search-depth check only, not exhaustive).

C2 -- REMOVE ENTANGLEMENT ITSELF (tests whether genuine entanglement,
not just classical correlation, is required for D0's conditioned
pattern (R01/R02) to show fringes at all -- whether fringes appear
there, and how strongly, is the open question itself). Swap the
BBO/SPDC crystal for a pseudo-thermal source (laser + rotating
ground-glass diffuser -- standard, 60-year-old technique, Martienssen &
Spiller, Am. J. Phys. 32, 919 (1964)), keeping the rest of the apparatus
(double slit, PS, BSa/BSb/BSc, D0-D4) unchanged. Two candidate
mechanisms, making distinguishable predictions:
  (i) CLASSICAL CORRELATION / ENSEMBLE AVERAGING: predicts REDUCED BUT
      NONZERO fringes in R01/R02. Modeling each slit region as an
      independent chaotic amplitude (mirroring SPDC's own "pair from
      EITHER region A OR region B" exclusivity) through Kim et al.'s own
      published D1/D3 topology (eqs. 4-5 of the original paper) gives a
      hard ceiling V=1/3~=33%, for D1/D2, independent of slit count --
      verified by direct Monte Carlo coincidence-counting simulation,
      6/6 PASS [analysis/quantum/
      c2_classical_correlation_visibility_bound.py]. Real-world
      imperfections would plausibly push an actual measurement below
      this ceiling, by an amount not derivable without choosing a
      specific implementation.
  (ii) TV-SPECIFIC ENTANGLEMENT-BREAKING ENERGY RELEASE: predicts a
      CLEAN ZERO -- no entanglement means no formation/breaking energy
      budget to perturb the downstream optics. Depends on two
      prerequisites not yet resolved for photon pairs specifically:
      does the A_g-singlet/G32-chain mechanism apply to theta=0
      particles at all, and what chain-breaking physically costs.
  KEY LOGICAL POINT: any nonzero fringes under C2 -- even the reduced
  1/3 of hypothesis (i) -- already refute the claim that entanglement
  is required for the fringe pattern to exist at all (call this Claim
  A) as completely as full visibility would; only a clean-zero result
  (hypothesis ii) leaves Claim A standing. A reduced-but-nonzero result
  narrows the live claim to entanglement being required only for
  FULL/undiminished magnitude (Claim B) -- narrower than this
  document's own Section 5.3 wording above currently implies (flagged
  for revision once C2 is actually run, or before any stronger claim
  than Claim B is asserted here).

C3 -- APPARATUS-REVERSAL-EQUIVALENT (tests whether the recombining
FUNCTION, not specific detector hardware/position, is what determines
which data set shows fringes; distinct from a literal detector swap,
which the original paper never performed -- confirmed directly from the
paper's own text, not a secondary source). Physically exchange which
output of BSa/BSb (transmitted vs. reflected) feeds the recombining
path (toward the shared mixing beamsplitter, currently BSc, currently
-> D1/D2) versus the direct path (currently -> D3/D4) -- using the same
physical elements, not new ones.
  EXPECTED outcome under the recombining mechanism itself, NOT a
  settled prediction (theory-agnostic -- standard QM and TV agree
  identically here, since both require an actual recombination event
  for interference): the fringe-carrying data set should follow the
  recombining FUNCTION, not physical hardware -- whichever detectors
  now perform the recombining role would show fringes, whichever now
  perform the direct role would not, regardless of which original
  hardware is involved. But this is exactly what the control needs to
  establish empirically, not assume -- a failure of this swap (fringes
  staying tied to original D1/D2 hardware/position, or appearing in
  neither/both) would be the important, diagnostic finding: evidence
  that something OTHER than erasure itself is responsible for the
  observed pattern.

STATUS: C1 and C3 are both theory-agnostic controls -- whatever either
one actually shows, standard QM and TV interpret it identically (they
do not predict different outcomes FROM EACH OTHER); their value is in
testing the experimental mechanism itself empirically, not in already
knowing the answer, and not in distinguishing torsionverse from
standard QM. C2 is the one control that could distinguish between two
live TV hypotheses, and under hypothesis (i) is already a quantitative,
falsifiable classical-vs-quantum discriminator independent of
torsionverse specifically. None of C1-C3 have been built. C2 hypothesis
(ii)'s own grounding (does entanglement even apply to photon pairs, what
does chain-breaking cost) remains open. Status: exploratory, under
review.
