SECTION 2: MECHANISM — CENTRIFUGAL T_1g CHI COUPLING ON THE C5 AXIS
-------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 WHY C5 ROTATION REDUCES T_1g PRESSURE ON THE AXIS

When the pyramid spins, centrifugal force creates an outward pressure gradient
in the medium. The direction and coherence of this gradient depend on the
rotational symmetry: chi(T_1g, C_N) determines whether the T_1g coupling
of the rotation is positive (reduces T_1g pressure on axis), negative (compresses axis), or
zero (no T_1g effect).

  chi(T_1g, C5) = +phi > 0:  centrifugal force creates constructive T_1g
                               pressure reduction on the axis. Object on
                               axis: reduced T_1g pressure, reduced apparent
                               weight. [SP1 PASS]

  chi(T_1g, C4) = -1   < 0:  centrifugal force compresses the axis (opposite
                               sign). Object on axis: increased T_1g pressure,
                               slight weight INCREASE, not decrease.

  chi(T_1g, C3) = 0:          no T_1g coupling to the rotation. No axis
                               pressure reduction or compression. Object: normal weight.

The sign flip between 4-sided (compresses) and 5-sided (reduces T_1g pressure) is the
strongest prediction: it is not that 4-sided works less — it works in the
opposite direction. This is a clean discriminator between the torsionverse
mechanism and any conventional fluid dynamic or aerodynamic explanation, which
would predict the same sign for all geometries at the same RPM.

2.2 SCALING WITH ANGULAR VELOCITY

The centrifugal force is F_centrifugal = m * omega^2 * r. The T_1g pressure
reduction on the spin axis scales proportionally to this force — at twice the angular
velocity the effect is four times as large. The apparent weight reduction
is therefore proportional to omega^2:

  delta_W / W proportional to omega^2   (quadratic RPM curve)

This is the same Omega^2 scaling derived independently for T_2g rotation in
the gyroscope companion paper [GY8]. The quadratic curve is the discriminating
measurement: a linear or cubic result would falsify the centrifugal T_1g
chi mechanism.

2.3 DYNAMIC VS PERMANENT EFFECT

The T_1g chi effect is dynamic: the reduced-pressure column is maintained only
while spinning. The medium returns to equilibrium when the pyramid stops, on
the acoustic timescale (~microseconds to milliseconds at lab scale), not the
slow tau_relax = 3.2 year quasi-static timescale.

This distinguishes the spinning pyramid from a hypothetical permanent pressure-
reduction device. The energy accounting is conventional: the motor pays for overcoming
aerodynamic drag and bearing friction. The medium provides the pressure
differential on the test mass at no additional energy cost beyond maintaining
the spin, because the medium's elastic restoring force is being continuously
overcome by centrifugal mechanics.


SECTION 3: EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL
----------------------------------

Full protocol: experiments/bench_pyramid_escalator.txt (Tests A-D)

EQUIPMENT:
  Pentagonal pyramid: face slope 31.72 deg, base ~10 cm, aluminium or brass.
  Motor: hobby brushless motor, variable speed, mounted above test platform.
  Scale: precision digital, 0.01 g resolution.
  Tachometer: optical or hall-effect, for RPM measurement.
  Total cost: < $30 in materials.

TEST A (baseline): Pyramid stationary above scale with test mass. Record
  baseline weight. Expected: normal weight (1.000 × baseline).

TEST B (weight vs RPM — primary test): Spin pyramid at 500, 1000, 2000, 3000
  RPM. Record apparent weight of test mass at each RPM. Plot delta_W/W vs
  omega^2. Expected: linear plot (weight reduction proportional to omega^2).

TEST C (orientation reversal — null control): Flip pyramid apex-down. Repeat
  Test B. Expected: no weight reduction, or slight weight increase. This
  controls for aerodynamic downwash (which acts downward regardless of
  orientation) and confirms the effect is geometric.

TEST D (shape comparison — discriminating test): Replace with 4-sided pyramid
  at same face slope 31.72 deg, same base size, same total mass. Repeat Test B.
  Expected: slight weight INCREASE on axis (chi = -1, compression).
  Replace with 3-sided: expected no effect (chi = 0).

DISC CONTROL: Spin a flat disc of the same total mass at the same RPM. Expected:
  negligible weight change. Confirms the effect requires pyramid geometry.

NOTE: face slope precision is less critical for the spinning test than for the
static escalator. The key parameter is the N-sided symmetry (C5 vs C4 vs C3)
and the RPM². A rough 5-sided pyramid will show the effect; a precise 31.72 deg
slope maximises it.

  But removing Ω₁ entirely would not eliminate forward thrust -- only reduce
  the per-arm coupling efficiency.

  Depletion coverage: Ω₂ sweeps the arm continuously; at sufficient RPM
  For continuous coverage of the forward disc, the condition is:
    (arm RPM × N_pyramids) × tau_cell ≥ 1
  i.e., the arm completes enough sweeps per medium relaxation time (tau_cell)
  that the forward region never fully re-fills between sweeps. This is the
  same saturation condition as SP3, but for the arm sweep rather than the
  face sweep. For tau_cell = 0.54 ns and N=5, the arm needs ~370 million RPM

  tau_cell which is unknown at lab scale.

  Counter-rotating arm pairs (Case B extended):
  Two arm assemblies counter-rotating (Ω₂ and −Ω₂): the arm sweep T_1g
  cancels across the pair → net T_1g from arm rotation = 0; T_2g² from
  arm sweep doubles. Each pyramid also counter-rotates on its own axis
  (+Ω₁ and −Ω₁). This gives FOUR chi contributions:
    Pyramid spin: T_1g cancels, T_2g² = 2/phi² (same as Case A)
    Arm sweep:    T_1g cancels, T_2g² = 2/phi² (from counter-rotating arms)
  Combined T_2g² = 4/phi² = 1.528 (stronger than Case A alone).
  This configuration creates a Protocol Beta field sweeping the entire
  forward disc — broader coverage than Case A, still free-fall for occupants.

  THE ENVELOPMENT QUESTION:
  Even with continuous coverage of the forward disc, the craft's body still
  passes through varying field strengths as arms sweep past. The solution is
  that the T_2g field (once established by the counter-rotating pairs) is
  volumetric — it fills the region ahead of the assembly, not just along
  arm paths. Individual arms contribute to the field; the field itself is
  the macroscopic T_2g field that envelops the craft's forward face.
  Components at the arm radius experience the strongest cycling; components
  at the craft center experience the average — which is the sustained T_1g
  reduction level. The craft interior is at the time-averaged field strength.

  Analogy to Protocol Beta: the fluid bell creates a volumetric T_2g field

  this with discrete arms rather than continuous shell geometry. As N → ∞

  discrete approximation (chi = phi, maximum coupling per arm).

  PRACTICAL NOTE: for an arm-based system to envelop a craft safely, the

  inside the swept disc. The craft is then consistently inside the low-pressure
