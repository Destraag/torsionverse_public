ABSTRACT
--------
In the torsionverse framework, a photon is a localized T_1g corpuscle that
travels at speed c through the Jobson cell medium. The local medium has a
characteristic frequency f_cell = c / L_J_local, where L_J_local is the
local Jobson cell spacing. What the observer measures is this local medium
frequency -- a medium property, not an intrinsic property of the corpuscle.
Because L_J ∝ n_cells^(-1/3) (cell spacing grows as cells become sparser),
the local medium frequency at a sparse void edge is lower than in a dense
filament:

  f_obs / f_emit = (n_void_edge / n_filament)^(1/3)
  z + 1 = (n_filament / n_void_edge)^(1/3)   [Candidate D, RS12]

The corpuscle speed (c) is unchanged. The scale argument rules out the
per-cell interaction picture: lambda_visible / L_J_dense ≈ 5×10^10. The
wave is fifty billion times larger than one cell; the medium is continuous
from the wave's perspective in both dense filaments and the deepest observed
voids (lambda / L_J_void ≈ 2×10^10 at 10% cell density). No individual cell
resonance is physically accessible to any observable photon. The mechanism
is a collective medium property, not a per-cell interaction.

f_cell = c/L_J_local is the local medium's characteristic frequency -- the
local propagation parameter set by cell spacing. The precise mechanism
connecting local cell density to spectroscopic redshift (boundary frequency
shift, accumulated phase, or medium clock rate) is an open derivation:
Series 3 target.

In sparse voids, ambient radiation pressure from all directions holds cells
at their equilibrium positions. This is a mechanical effect on cell location,
independent of EM wave propagation speed, which is set by eps_eff / mu_eff.
Radiation pressure is ~25 orders of magnitude weaker than EM stiffness
(P_CMB / (1/eps_0) ≈ 1e-25) and does not contribute to c.

This mechanism (a) is consistent with c constant everywhere (RS11),
(b) predicts a Hubble-tension correction of ~7-9% for the KBC void (25%
local underdensity), (c) predicts T_1g amplitude enhancement at cell density
boundaries -- consistent with unexplained heat observations at the heliosphere
boundary (RS13), and (d) identifies the Hubble radius c/H0 as the cell
propagation horizon beyond which no medium exists and therefore no structured
matter can survive (RS15).

The framework examines four candidate redshift mechanisms:
  (A) Standard metric expansion (currently imported from LambdaCDM, not derived)
  (B) "Local boson emission" (referenced elsewhere, never developed -- stale)
  (C) Pure cell migration outward -- examined here for the first time; ruled
      out as a creation mechanism but established as pure migration (RS7a)
  (D) Sparse void edges → lower local medium frequency → redshift --
      the primary new result; quantified with zero free parameters (RS12-RS15)

Beyond the cell propagation horizon, matter loses structural coherence (Hopf
windings require cells as substrate). Communication across the boundary remains
possible in principle if the sender corrects for the clock-rate shift, but
physical travel is prohibited by substrate dissolution, not by energy or speed.
All 20 checks pass [RS1-RS15] as exploratory framing checks; this paper is open
research, not a closed derivation of the cosmological constant or expansion rate.

(D) VOID PROPAGATION -- REDSHIFT FROM SPARSE VOID EDGE CELLS [new hypothesis]
    Physical picture: the KEY ZONE is the void EDGE: where cell density

    propagates through a medium with increasing L_J_local, and the observer
    in dense medium interprets the longer-scale T_1g disturbance as
    longer wavelength = lower frequency = REDSHIFT.

      In dense medium: local medium characteristic period τ_dense = L_J / c
      In sparse void edge (density n_void_edge): L_J_void = L_J × (n_dense/n_void_edge)^(1/3)
        τ_void = L_J_void / c = τ_dense × (n_dense/n_void_edge)^(1/3)
      The sparse-edge medium has a longer characteristic period (slower clock).
      Photons emitted in (or passing through) the slow-clock region appear
      redshifted when measured by the observer's faster dense-region clock:

        z + 1 = τ_void / τ_dense = (n_dense / n_void_edge)^(1/3)

      RS11 COMPATIBILITY: c is unchanged.
      The redshift arises from the local clock rate (medium characteristic period),
      not from a change in propagation speed. RS11 applies to speed; this
      mechanism applies to the ratio of local to observer clock rates.

    CONSEQUENCE: HARD PHYSICAL BOUNDARY FOR MATTER AND TRAVEL
    The cell propagation horizon (c/H0 ≈ 14.5 Gly, RS15) is not merely an
    information horizon — it is a hard physical substrate boundary.

    Matter in torsionverse = Hopf windings in Jobson cells (Series 1, derived).
    Photons = localized T_1g wave packets propagating through the Jobson cell medium (derived).
    Both require cells to exist. Beyond the cell horizon: no cells → no medium
    → no Hopf windings → matter cannot maintain structural coherence.

    An electron approaching the cell boundary would find progressively fewer
    cells to sustain its (1,2) winding. At the boundary, the winding unravels:
    the energy stored in the winding is no longer a stable topological
    configuration — there is nothing to wind around. The electron ceases to
    exist as an electron. Its constituent energy dissipates into... nothing that
    any medium-based physics can describe.

    This is fundamentally different from a Schwarzschild event horizon:
      - GR event horizon: matter can cross (in principle); only communication
        is cut. The boundary is a coordinate singularity.
      - Cell propagation horizon: matter CANNOT maintain structure beyond the
        boundary. The boundary is a physical substrate limit.

    Safe travel implication: no craft can travel beyond the cell horizon and
    remain as structured matter. The cells that constitute the craft — and the
    Hopf windings (atoms, molecules) within it — would lose their substrate.
    This is not a velocity or energy limit; it is a structural impossibility.
    The universe has a hard boundary not because of curvature but because the
    medium that makes matter possible simply ends there.

    FERMI PARADOX COROLLARY:
    Every civilization in the observable universe faces the same hard physical
    boundary for matter travel. No craft — regardless of technology — can
    cross the cell propagation horizon and remain as structured matter.
    If other cell islands exist beyond our Hubble radius, their matter cannot
    physically reach ours.

    However, COMMUNICATION across cell density gradients is not absolutely
    forbidden. A civilization that understands the Candidate D clock-rate
    mechanism (z + 1 = (n_dense/n_void)^(1/3)) could pre-compensate their
    signal: by adjusting the transmission frequency upward by the void clock
    factor, the signal arrives at the observer's end at the correct local
    frequency. Cross-island communication would require:
      (1) Knowledge of the cell density structure between sender and receiver
      (2) A signal channel that survives propagation through sparse cell regions
          (T_2g gravitational waves propagate through sparser medium than T_1g)
      (3) Patience: round-trip communication time = 2 × distance / c

    The silence from beyond our cell island may reflect the bootstrap problem
    (two sides must agree on the correction factor before either can send a
    correctable signal), not a fundamental prohibition on communication.
    Within our shared cell region, standard Fermi constraints still apply
    locally (distance, time, RS13 void signal attenuation).
