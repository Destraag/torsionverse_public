ABSTRACT
--------

Matter follows pressure gradients in the torsion medium. This single
mechanism -- the same one that produces gravity and electromagnetism -- also
explains acoustic phenomena at human scales. Chladni patterns, acoustic
levitation, and natural geometric resonance in rocks and structures are all
manifestations of the same T_1g pressure-following law. These are not
analogies: they ARE the torsion medium mechanism operating at different scales.

Certain geometric configurations in nature -- crystal structures, enclosed
cavities, ring arrangements, and apex shapes -- focus T_1g pressure and create
low-pressure zones. Objects in those zones experience reduced effective gravity.
This may explain anomalous lightness in ringing rocks, acoustic phenomena at
megalithic stone circles, and levitation effects in natural vortices.


1. CHLADNI PATTERNS: DIRECT EVIDENCE OF PRESSURE-FOLLOWING
------------------------------------------------------------

When a plate is driven at resonance, it forms standing waves. Sand or rice
grains sprinkled on the plate migrate to the nodal lines (pressure nodes of
the standing wave) and form the famous Chladni figures.

TORSIONVERSE MECHANISM: Sand grains drift to pressure nodes by dwell-time
asymmetry -- the same mechanism that produces gravity. Near a pressure node,
the wave spends more time (longer dwell time) per unit displacement; grains
accumulate where dwell time is maximum. This is not radiation pressure
(which pushes grains to pressure antinodes); it is the torsionverse
pressure-following mechanism, which moves matter to pressure MINIMA.

Note: Standard acoustic radiation pressure theory predicts grains move to
pressure antinodes for objects larger than the wavelength/2, and to nodes
for smaller objects. The torsionverse provides the underlying mechanism
for the "smaller object" case (dwell-time asymmetry) and connects it to
gravity and EM through the same pressure-following law.

Prediction: Chladni patterns driven by two sources at 72 degrees should show

2. ACOUSTIC LEVITATION
-----------------------

Acoustic levitation uses standing waves to hold small objects in mid-air at
pressure nodes. Standard explanation: radiation pressure balances gravity.

TORSIONVERSE MECHANISM: Objects drift to pressure minima by dwell-time
asymmetry. In a vertical standing wave, the pressure nodes are fixed points
where the wave is stationary. Objects accumulate there because their local
dwell-time gradient is zero (stable equilibrium) -- the same reason a
planetary orbit is stable.

TORSIONVERSE PREDICTION:
(a) The levitation force scales with T_1g coupling efficiency of the medium
    at the crossing angle. A 72-deg crossing geometry should levitate objects
    with phi^2 = 2.618x less required acoustic power than a 90-deg geometry.
(b) Objects aligned with icosahedral cell axes (as set by local proton
    chirality) will levitate with lower required field than randomly oriented
    objects.

------------------------------------------------------

The coupling strength at the focus of any geometry is given by chi(T_1g)
evaluated at the dominant rotation angle (I_h character table, Cotton 1990):
  chi(T_1g, 72 deg,  C5) =  phi = 1.618  [maximum in I_h]
  chi(T_1g, 120 deg, C3) =  0             [exact null]
  chi(T_1g, 180 deg, C2) = -1             [destructive]
  N-ring (adjacent spacing): chi = 1 + 2*cos(360/N)
    N=3 -> 0  |  N=5 -> phi  |  N=10 -> phi^2  |  N->inf -> 3 (full circle)
  N=4 RING (adjacent 90 deg): chi = +1 (positive).
  4-sided PYRAMID (opposite faces 180 deg): chi = -1.
  Same count, different dominant angle, different sign.

Any geometric arrangement that focuses T_1g (pressure) mode waves to a region
creates a pressure minimum there. Coupling efficiency at the focus depends on
the symmetry of the arrangement via chi(T_1g) at the relevant rotation angle.
Objects in the low-pressure zone experience reduced effective gravity. The
engineered pyramid is one instance; natural equivalents exist across many scales.

3.1 CRYSTAL RESONANCE IN ROCKS -- THE RINGING STONES MECHANISM

Some rocks produce a clear musical tone when struck ("ringing rocks," "singing
stones"): Ringing Rocks Park (Pennsylvania), bluestones at Stonehenge, various
megalithic sites globally. Standard geology notes these tend to be high-silica
rocks with olivine or pyroxene inclusions.

TORSIONVERSE MECHANISM:
  - The rock's size and crystal domain structure set its resonance frequencies
  - Olivine/pyroxene inclusions have approximate icosahedral packing in their
    atomic coordination shells → T_1g mode couples at those frequencies
  - Seismic background noise (Schumann resonances 7-45 Hz, microseisms 0.1-0.3 Hz)
    continuously drives the rock at its natural resonance
  - At resonance: internal standing wave creates a T_1g pressure pattern
  - Pressure minimum inside → the rock is lighter than a non-resonant rock
    of the same material and size

PREDICTIONS:
  (a) Ringing rocks are measurably lighter per unit volume than non-ringing
      rocks of the same parent material and size. Weight deficit ~ Q factor
      of the resonance.
  (b) A ringing rock driven at its resonance by an external acoustic source
      shows measurable apparent weight reduction on a precision scale.
      Effect is fully reversible when driving stops (dynamic eddy, not a
      T_2g vortex; relaxes on acoustic timescale, not tau_relax = 10^8 s).
  (c) Megalithic builders may have selected ringing stones partly because they
      were lighter to transport (Stonehenge bluestones chosen from Preseli Hills,
      Wales -- these ring when struck; the 250 km transport then makes more sense).

3.2 APEX GEOMETRIES IN NATURE

Any tapered shape focuses arriving T_1g waves to its tip, creating a pressure
minimum at the apex. Crystal symmetry of the material determines coupling
efficiency:

  - Quartz crystal terminations: hexagonal prism + rhombohedral cap (C3 symmetry).
    C3 not in I_h → chi(T_1g,C3) = 0. Zero crystal coupling; geometric focus only.
  - Calcite stalagmites / stalactites: trigonal (C3) symmetry. Same: zero crystal
    coupling, geometric apex effect only.
  - Olivine (orthorhombic, C2v): some C2 coupling: chi(T_1g,C2) = -1.
    Slight destructive coupling. Olivine-bearing tapers are marginally worse
    than amorphous.
  - Pyrite (FeS2): despite pseudo-icosahedral reputation, crystal symmetry is
    O_h (cubic), not I_h. C4/C2 crossing → chi(T_1g,C2) = -1. Suboptimal.

NOTE: No common natural crystal achieves C5 coupling in a single-crystal
pyramid. C5 coupling requires I_h geometry -- the Jobson cell itself. Natural
materials approximate C5 only in disordered or quasicrystalline forms
(Icosahedrite, Al-Mn quasicrystal).

3.3 ENCLOSED CAVITIES: GEODES AND STONE CHAMBERS

A closed or near-closed cavity creates internal standing waves at its resonant
frequencies. At those frequencies, the center of a spherical or dome-shaped
cavity becomes a T_1g pressure minimum -- the acoustic analogue of a Fabry-Perot
resonator.

  - Geodes (hollow mineral nodules): roughly spherical quartz or calcite shell.
    At resonance, the interior center is a pressure minimum. A geode at acoustic
    resonance is lighter than the same geode off-resonance.

  - Corbelled stone chambers (Newgrange, Irish passage tombs, Mycenaean tholos
    tombs): domed interior has a well-defined acoustic resonance. Seismic driving
    creates a chronic low-pressure zone at the focal center. This may be the
    functional basis for the dome geometry.
    Computed for Newgrange (R~3 m chamber): f = v/2R = 57 Hz (spherical approx);
    actual corbelled geometry has R_eff ~ 1.56 m giving f ~ 110 Hz (consistent
    with published archaeoacoustics; Watson & Keating 1999).
    Helmholtz mode of the 19 m passage + chamber: f = 1.18 Hz (deep infrasound).

  - Helmholtz resonator caves: large cave interior with narrow entrance neck.
    At f = (v/2pi)*sqrt(A/(L*V)), interior pressure is a minimum relative to
    outside. Objects inside experience slightly reduced effective gravity at
    that frequency.

3.4 RING GEOMETRIES: STONE CIRCLES AS STATIC CAROUSELS

A ring of N upright stones with vertical long axes acts as the static version
of the spinning N-pyramid carousel. Driving is seismic and acoustic background
rather than centrifugal. N-dependence is identical:

  N=3 (triangle of 3 stones): chi(T_1g,C3) = 0 -- null at center
  N=4 (square of 4 stones):   chi(T_1g,C2) = -1 -- destructive at center
  N=5 (pentagon of 5 stones): chi(T_1g,C5) = phi -- maximum at center

  STONEHENGE (horseshoe of 5 trilithons): approximate C5 symmetry, opening
  toward midsummer sunrise. The altar stone sits at the focal point -- where
  the T_1g pressure minimum would be for a C5 ring. If intentional, this is
  the optimal ring configuration from I_h character theory.

PREDICTION: Stone circles with N=5 (or N=10, 15) show stronger documented
acoustic and anomalous phenomena at their centers than N=3 or N=4 circles.

3.5 VORTEX GEOMETRIES

A rotational fluid vortex has a low-pressure axis. In the torsionverse, a
sustained vortex is a T_2g (shear/magnetic) mode excitation -- distinct from
the T_1g geometric focus of the geometries above.

  - Tornado eye: very low atmospheric pressure, low wind at center. Reports of
    objects rising inside tornado eyes may include a T_2g component. T_2g vortex
    tau_relax >= 10^8 s in matter-free voids -- the eye can sustain reduced
    effective gravity along its axis beyond what aerodynamics explains.
  - Whirlpool center: same mechanism at smaller scale.

NOTE: T_2g (vortex) and T_1g (geometric focus) are complementary but distinct.
Pyramids, rings, and cavities operate by T_1g focusing. Vortices operate by
T_2g shear depletion. Both create low-pressure on axis; different geometries
produce them.
