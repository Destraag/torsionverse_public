ABSTRACT
--------

This paper derives four mechanisms for torsionverse-enhanced bond disruption and
water splitting. (1) IRMPD: baseline sequential T_1g excitation, 11.2 photons
per O-H bond. (2a) A_g orthogonal excitation at 90 deg: T_1g x T_1g = A_g + T_1g
+ H_g [exact, I_h CG] gives single-step direct excitation of the O-H symmetric
stretch. (2b) C5 enhancement at 72 deg: chi(T_1g, C5) = phi gives phi^2 = 2.618x
more A_g per unit intensity than 90 deg, reducing the effective photon count from
11.2 to 4.3 per bond [CC8]; possibly also includes I_h medium coupling (open).
(3) Pulsed oversize toroidal B-field: creates T_2g vortex in the medium, depleting
the Zone 2 pressure well and reducing the effective bond dissociation energy.
(4) Photonic ring resonator / annular EM focus: circulating T_1g TE_01 mode
achieves milliwatt CW bond excitation [BD8-BD9] or pulsed annular focus with
IRMPD window confirmed at R=1.5 um [BD12-BD13].
Section 5b derives the torsionverse mechanism for covalent bonding: the photon
in a closed (1,2) trajectory has a 2-cell displacement budget (from linking number
n = p*q = 2, CC12), explaining the 2-center preference, Pauli exclusion, and
electron degeneracy pressure from a single first principle.
The 72 deg / phi^2 prediction is falsifiable: two OPO lasers at 2.70 um;
measure bond-breaking yield at 72 deg vs 90 deg; expected ratio = phi^2.

NOTE ON PRIOR ART: General crossed-beam IR photodissociation is published
(IRMPD, 2D-IR spectroscopy). The 72 deg / phi^2 prediction from I_h group
theory is new. No existing framework predicts the specific enhancement ratio
or the group-theoretic origin of the optimal angle.

Magnetic pre-alignment (see Section 1b) enhances efficiency of Mechanisms 2a and
2b by orienting water's dipole moment (1.85 Debye) before the excitation pulse: all molecules
present the O-H bond at the same angle, enabling coherent (not statistical)
excitation. The magnetic pulse must be shorter than the O-H coherence time
(sub-picosecond) to avoid decoherence while achieving bond-pressure alteration.

1.1  The O-H bond as a torsion pressure well

  Bond energy:      E_OH = 5.15 eV  [NIST]

  O-H stretch:      nu = 3700 cm^-1  -->  h*nu = 0.4587 eV,  lambda = 2.70 um
  Zone 2 pressure at bond scale:  P = E_OH / V_bond = 224 GPa  [BD1 PASS]

  The O-H bond is a Zone 2 pressure well at molecular scale. The same Zone 2
  physics that holds quarks inside a proton (at r ~ lambda_p = 0.21 fm) holds
  the O-H electrons between the oxygen and hydrogen nuclei (at r ~ 0.958 A).
  The scale difference is 4.5 orders of magnitude; the mechanism is identical.

1.2  Bond vibrational modes and I_h symmetry

  The O-H stretch frequency (111 THz) is the A_g mode of the O-H
  bond at molecular scale. A_g is the totally symmetric I_h irreducible
  representation -- an isotropic radial oscillation of the bond's Zone 2
  pressure well (all directions simultaneously, no preferred axis). In
  standard spectroscopy this is the symmetric stretch (nu_1); in the
  torsionverse it IS the A_g mode of the local I_h pressure geometry.

  TORSIONVERSE MECHANISM FOR nu_1:
    (1) T_1g pressure at the bond locus drops isotropically (A_g reduction)
        -- the Zone 2 pressure well shallows uniformly in all directions.
    (2) The electron (E+ vertex mode) follows the pressure minimum outward
        by dwell-time asymmetry -- it drifts away from the bond centre.
    (3) T_1g pressure restores -- the well deepens again.
    (4) Electron rebounds to equilibrium.
    Repeat at 111 THz. This is nu_1: periodic isotropic pressure oscillation
    of the bond well, with the electron tracking the pressure gradient.

  WHY A_g EXCITATION BREAKS THE BOND:
    At sufficient amplitude, the pressure well drops enough that the electron
    drifts too far to rebound -- it escapes the Zone 2 pressure minimum and
    the bond dissociates. No collision or photon energy threshold required;
    the bond breaks when the isotropic pressure reduction exceeds the well depth.

  CONNECTION TO CROSSED-BEAM MECHANISM (Section 3):
    Two T_1g beams crossing at 90 deg create a local T_1g pressure reduction
    at the crossing point. This IS an A_g perturbation (isotropic, no preferred
    direction) -- it couples directly into the nu_1 oscillation without requiring
    the sequential ladder that IRMPD uses.

  A_g at cell scale is the Higgs/cell isotropic mode (E_cell = 124.8 GeV);
  A_g at bond scale is the O-H symmetric stretch (0.46 eV). Same irrep,
  of an icosahedral elastic structure, differing only in the scale of the
  enclosing geometry.

  H-O-H bend:        nu_2 = 1595 cm^-1  -->  T_1g symmetry (directed)
  O-H stretch (symm): nu_1 = 3657 cm^-1  -->  A_g symmetry (isotropic)
  O-H stretch (asymm): nu_3 = 3756 cm^-1 -->  T_1g symmetry (directed)

  Key: IRMPD targets the A_g mode (nu_1) because it builds amplitude without
  directionality. The asymmetric stretch (nu_3, T_1g) is less efficient for

1b. MAGNETIC PRE-ALIGNMENT (NEW INGREDIENT)

  Water molecules have a permanent electric dipole moment: mu = 1.85 Debye.
  In an external magnetic or electric field, the molecular dipole aligns with
  the field. When aligned, every O-H bond in the sample presents at the same
  angle to the excitation beam -- coherent excitation becomes possible instead
  of the statistical averaging that reduces efficiency in unaligned samples.

  PULSE REQUIREMENTS:
    Alignment field: applied BEFORE the excitation pulse
    Duration: must be longer than the rotational relaxation time of water
      (tau_rot ~ 8 ps at 300 K) to achieve alignment, but the EXCITATION
      pulse must arrive BEFORE thermal disruption restores random orientation.
    Excitation pulse: sub-picosecond (shorter than O-H coherence time ~100 fs)
      to achieve coherent A_g excitation without decoherence from thermal noise.
    Sequence: align (ns field pulse) -> excite (fs laser pulse) -> measure

  IN TORSIONVERSE TERMS: the external field creates a preferred orientation
  for the medium's local cell structure around each molecule. When the
  excitation beams arrive, every A_g mode is in phase (all bonds at the
  same angle to the beam crossing point). The phi^2 enhancement from the
  72-degree geometry is MULTIPLIED by the coherence enhancement from alignment
  (factor ~N_aligned/N_total → approaches 1.0 for strong alignment fields).

  PRACTICAL GEOMETRY:
    Field: ~1-10 T magnetic field or ~10^6 V/m electric field (strong, pulsed)
    Laser: two OPO beams at 2.70 um (111 THz), crossed at 72 deg
    Target: water film or droplet at the field/beam crossing point
    Observable: bond-breaking yield vs angle (72 vs 90 vs 0 vs 180 deg)


2. MECHANISM 1: IRMPD SEQUENTIAL LADDER (EXISTING, SCRIPTED)
--------------------------------------------------------------

  IR multiphoton dissociation: photons at 2.70 um resonance excite the O-H
  bond sequentially. Each absorbed photon climbs one rung of the vibrational
  ladder. N photons needed:

    N = E_bond / h*nu = 5.15 eV / 0.4587 eV = 11.2 photons  [BD5 PASS]

  This is the TRUE multi-photon regime (N >> 1). Each photon must be absorbed
  before the next arrives; coherence is not required. The mechanism is:
    1. Photon absorbed by O-H stretch mode (A_g, 3700 cm^-1)
    2. Bond amplitude increases
    3. Repeat N=11 times
    4. Bond breaks at the dissociation threshold

  SOURCE REQUIREMENTS:
    Single-photon DUV path: lambda < 240 nm; E > 5.15 eV per photon  [BD4 PASS]
    IRMPD at 2.70 um: requires 11.2 photons, < 5 ps pulse for coherence  [BD5]
    Brute-force field: I > 10^14 W/cm^2 -- exceeds ionization threshold  [BD2 PASS]
    Anti-Helmholtz static B: gradient force negligible  (F/F_bond ~ 4e-17)  [BD6]
    Diamagnetic force: F_dia ~ 1e-3 pN  <<  F_bond = 8.6 nN  [BD7 PASS]

  PRACTICAL APPROACH: CO2 laser (10.6 um) is NOT resonant with O-H stretch.
  Recommended: OPO source at 2.70 um, focused to I ~ 1e10 W/cm^2.
  Ring cavity enhancement (Q ~ 1e5) reduces required source power by 10^5x.
  [BD8-BD15 PASS in bond_disruption_em.py]

  MEDIUM SHEAR RESONANCE: the cell shear mode at 19.76 THz = Rs * f_photon
  (Rs = sqrt(5)/(4*pi)) provides a secondary coupling channel directly to
  cell displacement. Combined O-H + shear drive:
    f_OH    = 111 THz (bond climbing)
    f_shear = 19.76 THz (cell displacement)
  Both can be driven simultaneously with a two-colour field.  [JD12 PASS]

  ADVANTAGE OVER IRMPD:

  BEAM CONFIGURATION:

3b. MECHANISM 3: PULSED OVERSIZE TOROIDAL MAGNETIC FIELD -- MEDIUM DEPLETION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  This mechanism does NOT work by coupling to the bond directly (diamagnetic
  gradient force is negligible: F_dia/F_bond ~ 1e-17 [BD7]). It works by
  depleting the torsion medium at the toroid axis.

  PHYSICAL SETUP:
    An oversize toroidal electromagnet -- large enough for water to flow through
    the center hole -- is pulsed rapidly. The pulsed B-field (T_2g mode in the
    torsionverse) creates a sustained T_2g vortex in the torsion medium along
    the toroid axis. This depletes medium cells from the axis, lowering the local
    T_1g (EM/pressure) density in the region where the water flows.

  MECHANISM: MEDIUM DEPLETION, NOT BOND FORCE
    The torsionverse bond model: O-H bonds are Zone 2 pressure wells sustained
    by the surrounding medium's T_1g pressure field. The Zone 3 co-rotation of
    oxygen and hydrogen sets the dwell-time minimum that defines the bond.
    In a medium-depleted region (fewer cells → lower T_1g pressure):
      - Zone 3 orbital pressure gradient weakens
      - The effective bond dissociation energy decreases
      - Bonds that are thermally stable in full medium may dissociate spontaneously
        or break at significantly lower IRMPD fluence

    This is the same principle as the spinning pyramid reducing apparent weight:
    centrifugal T_1g depletion on the spin axis lowers effective gravity for
    objects there. Here, T_2g vortex depletion at the toroid axis lowers
    effective bond stability for molecules there.

  COMPARISON TO MECHANISM 2 (orthogonal beams):
    Both mechanisms reduce the effective bond stability by modifying the medium.
    - Mechanism 2: T_1g orthogonal beams create an A_g excitation that directly
      drives the bond's Zone 2 pressure well into A_g oscillation from ABOVE (added energy into the bond)
    - Mechanism 3: T_2g toroidal vortex removes medium pressure support from
      BELOW (reduces the pressure well that the bond sits in)
    The two are complementary and can be combined: weaken the bond via toroid,
    then break it with IRMPD or A_g beams at a fraction of the normal fluence.

  ADVANTAGE:
    The toroid is azimuthally symmetric; no critical crossing angle.
    Water flows continuously through the axis (scalable throughput).
    The depletion effect persists for the medium relaxation timescale after
    the pulse ends (not tau_relax = 1e8 s; this is elastic medium response,
    which relaxes on acoustic timescales when the driver stops).

5b. THE COVALENT BOND: WHY ELECTRON SHARING IS STABLE
-------------------------------------------------------

  Standard quantum mechanics resolves covalent bonding via wavefunction overlap
  and exchange integrals, but provides no mechanical picture. The torsionverse
  gives a direct mechanical explanation from a single fundamental object.

  THE FUNDAMENTAL OBJECT: THE PHOTON
    In the torsionverse there is one fundamental moving object: the photon.
    What we call the "electron" is a photon in a stable closed trajectory
    through the Jobson cell lattice. The (1,2) topology is NOT an intrinsic
    property of the photon -- it is a property of the TRAJECTORY through the
    icosahedral cell geometry. The photon bounces in straight lines between
    cells; the icosahedral lattice forces specific stable closed trajectories;
    the (1,2) trajectory is one such stable loop.

  THE 2-CELL BUDGET (from trajectory geometry):
    Linking number n = p*q = 2 counts simultaneous transverse crossings. [CC12a]
    Dynamic picture: the electron ricochets off a DIFFERENT cell at each bounce
    with an elastic rebound -- the cell displaces and recovers under global medium
    pressure. The electron travels to the next cell while the previous cell is still
    displaced. If each cell is displaced for 2 bounce intervals (tau_recover ~ T/6,
    where T/12 is the inter-bounce interval), then at any moment ~2 cells are
    simultaneously still displaced from recent impacts. Both the topological count
    (n=2 crossings) and the dynamic picture (2 cells displaced) give the same
    budget: 2 cells.
    Over a complete cycle: 12 distinct cells visited, 6 sequential 2-cell pairs.
    [CC12b, CC12c]. With vertex stiffness correction: n_exact = 2.018697.
    For all bond discussions: the budget is 2 cells.

  ENERGY BUDGET (2-cell budget):
    The photon's trajectory uses its 2-cell budget:
      ~1 cell: the trajectory presses into the nucleus Zone 3 cell geometry
               (the nuclear charge well is a specific region of the cell lattice)
      ~1 cell: the trajectory's orbital motion + Pauli repulsion capacity
    PAULI EXCLUSION: a second photon on the same trajectory would double the
    cell pressure → their trajectories repel each other unless the second photon
    circles the same path in the anti-parallel orientation (same cells visited,
    winding axis reversed). Anti-parallel: the two T_1g circulations cancel via
    A_g decoupling (chi(T_1g_up × T_1g_down, C_2) = +1 = A_g) → coexist.
    Parallel: circulations reinforce → repulsion → excluded.
    NOTE: opposite winding chirality = POSITRON (same cells, reversed order =
    anti-particle). Pauli pairs are NOT positrons -- they have anti-parallel
    axes, not reversed chirality.

  COVALENT BOND (two-center):
    When a second nucleus is close enough, the photon's closed trajectory can
    include cells from BOTH nuclear lattice regions -- alternating between
    nucleus A's Zone 3 cells and nucleus B's Zone 3 cells. The trajectory
    splits its 2-cell budget: ~1 cell in A's region, ~1 cell in B's region.
    Zero budget remains for orbital motion or Pauli repulsion. The trajectory
    is LOCKED to the path that spans both nuclei. This is the bond.

  WHY EXACTLY 2 CENTERS (not 3):
    The 2-cell budget supports exactly 2 nuclear cell regions. A trajectory
    spanning 3 nuclei would need n > 2 -- no stable (p,q) winding achieves this
    with p*q = 2. The 2-center preference is a direct consequence of the
    same topology that fixes alpha (n*alpha^2 - Q*alpha + Rs = 0).

  RESOLUTION OF THE "OVERLY POSITIVE" PROBLEM:
    There is no charge redistribution. The photon's trajectory spans two nuclear
    cell regions; both nuclei are attracted toward the region where their cell
    geometries overlap (the trajectory's Zone 3 path through both). The Coulomb
    repulsion between bare nuclei at H-H bond distance is ~19.5 eV [CC11], but
    the Zone 3 overlap attraction is larger; net bond energy 4.52 eV is the residual.

  PAULI EXCLUSION AND CELL DEPLETION:
    Pauli exclusion works because the photon trajectory requires spare cell budget
    to repel a co-entering photon. In a depleted medium (fewer Jobson cells),
    the spare budget falls → exclusion fails → trajectories can overlap.
    This is electron degeneracy pressure: at high stellar density, cell depletion
    removes the spare budget → Pauli repulsion fails → collapse.

    Preferred trajectory directions in the icosahedral cell lattice →

    TOPOLOGICAL (exact): the (1,2) trajectory simultaneously crosses any transverse
