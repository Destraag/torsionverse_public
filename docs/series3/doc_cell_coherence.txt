ABSTRACT
--------
The Jobson cell (I_h icosahedron, Series 1) is treated as a rigid unit in
all Series 1 and Series 2 derivations. This paper provides the quantitative
justification: (1) the bilateral tau (I52) synchronizes all 20 face-center gluon
maxima in 5.39 × L_J/c = 1.79×10^-25 s — shorter than ANY physical interaction
timescale at sub-E_cell energies; (2) the Maxwell-critical lattice (3V-E=6) has zero-energy soft
modes and cannot absorb wave energy as heat; (3) both results break down at
exactly E_cell = 124.8 GeV — the Nyquist condition where the photon wavelength
equals L_J. At this frequency, the wave couples to the cell's A_g (Higgs)
mode rather than propagating through it. The Higgs mass IS the UV cutoff of
the torsion medium, derived from wave mechanics rather than spontaneous
symmetry breaking alone. Three consequences follow: lossless EM propagation
below E_cell, dynamic rigidity for all nuclear and atomic processes, and a
clean boundary above which cell-scale physics is directly accessible.
Exceptions (Pauli-locked states, Zone-1-excited configurations) are noted as
open items.


SECTION 1: THE COHERENCE QUESTION
-----------------------------------

Series 1 treats each Jobson cell as a rigid unit — a solid icosahedral object
that rotates, translates, and locks with neighbours via the co-rotation chain
(cell_rotation_propagation.py RP1-RP4). This is the "geared ball" picture.

The cell's structural modes are built from three classes of corpuscle. The
gluons (2G_g) are STANDING oscillations anchored at each of the 30 edges —
three per triangular face, together constituting that face [GH0c, FG3-FG8]. 
The muon (G32) is a TRAVELING wave through the edge network, 
using the gluon-defined face boundaries as
waveguides. The tau (I52) makes a Hamiltonian circuit of all 20 face-center nexuses
(at inradius = 0.756 × L_J on the outer surface). Between nexuses the tau
travels as a straight-line CHORD through the cell interior — not a surface-
following path. At mid-hop the chord dips to r = 0.706 × L_J, which is
0.103 × L_J inside the edge midpoint (r_mid = 0.809 × L_J); the path
passes beneath the edge, so no surface-clearing is required. Each hop length
is phi/3 × L_J ≈ 0.54 × L_J [GH3]. At each face-center nexus all three
gluons of that face reach their standing-wave amplitude maximum simultaneously
[GH0b] — the tau arrives from the interior, scatters off this inward-facing
gluon maximum, and deflects by 72° (C5, same as the muon at vertices)
[GH2, GH5]. The 138.19° dihedral (JC5) is the fold of the cell surface at
the edge each chord crosses — geometry of the shell, not the tau's angle.
"Outer surface only" refers to the NEXUSES (all 20 at inradius); the path
segments pass through the interior. No inward-spiraling tau path exists
[CE1-CE5 — a separate topology where tau winds toward r=0, forbidden].
When an external force is applied — say, a proton's Zone 3 co-rotation
locking a cell face — all these internal modes must receive and transmit
the perturbation coherently. If they don't, the cell "flexes" rather than
rotates as a unit, and the rigid-body treatment breaks down.

The question is not whether the cell is in static equilibrium (proved by the
zero-force checks at all 62 nexuses, jobson_cell_force_balance_vectors.py G3D1-G3D8).
The question is whether the cell responds DYNAMICALLY as a rigid unit when
external forces are applied at any sub-E_cell energy.

This paper proves it does, and identifies E_cell as the exact energy where
the rigid-body picture ends.


SECTION 2: DYNAMIC RIGIDITY — THE TIMESCALE ARGUMENT [CC1-CC3]
----------------------------------------------------------------

The cell's internal coherence is maintained by the bilateral tau (I52) — TWO corpuscle
photons traversing the complete Hamiltonian circuit of all 20 face-center nexuses in
opposite directions [GH5]. At each nexus all three gluons of that face reach their
standing-wave amplitude maximum simultaneously [GH0b], so each tau hop visits one
face's full gluon state. Each photon makes 10 hops before meeting its partner at the
antipodal nexus (hop 10); together they have visited all 20 face-center gluon maxima.
Hop length = phi/3 × L_J [GH3]. The full-cell gluon synchronization time is:

  t_tau_sync = 10 × (phi/3 × L_J)/c = 5.39 × L_J/c = 1.79×10^-25 s   [CC1]

Compare to physical interaction timescales:

  Strong force at r_p:   t_strong = r_p / c = 2.80×10^-24 s  = 15.7× t_tau_sync
  EM at nuclear scale:   t_EM     = r_p/(c*alpha) = 3.84×10^-22 s  = 2,150× t_tau_sync
  Atomic scale:          t_atom   ~ 10^-17 s               = 10^8× t_tau_sync

For EVERY physical interaction below E_cell, the bilateral tau has visited all
20 face-center gluon maxima before the external force can change meaningfully.
The cell therefore responds as a coherent unit for all sub-E_cell interactions.

The co-rotation chain (RP1-RP4) confirms zero attenuation: each handoff is
exactly 72° (C5 geometry), and the muon's pole-to-pole zigzag [muon_symmetry.py MS1-MS7]
delivers the signal to all 12 vertices equally (35 circuits/vertex over the 70
distinct circuits). No corpuscle is left behind.

The tau-lever synchronization is Bode-stable: a gluon phase offset δ at a nexus
generates a restoring force ∝ (1−cos δ) [GPS1-GPS4], giving second-order (δ²)
stability. The per-circuit loop gain is G_total = 3.75×10⁻⁴ — a 2,670× damping
factor per tau traversal [BS1-BS4]. Any phase error is suppressed within a
single circuit. [GPS1-GPS6, BS1-BS5]

CONCLUSION: The "geared ball" treatment is JUSTIFIED for all physical processes
below E_cell. The rigid-body approximation is not an assumption — it follows
from the bilateral tau synchronizing all face gluons in 1.79×10^-25 s, which is
15.7× shorter than the strong force timescale. [CC2]


SECTION 2a: CORPUSCLE CYCLE TIMING AND PERTURBATION RECOVERY [CC_G1-CC_T4, STAB_G1-STAB_T3]
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The three corpuscle types have distinct cycle periods, verified without wave
mathematics by cell_cycle_corpuscle.py and cell_cycle_stability.py:

  Gluon (2 counter-propagating corpuscles per edge, 60 total):
    Period = 2 edge-crossings — corpuscle bounces between two antipodal
    vertices; the two corpuscles per edge are always at opposite vertices
    (standing-wave pattern). [CC_G1, CC_G2, CC_G3]

  Muon (G32, 20 bilateral corpuscles = 10 orbit-A circuits):
    Period = 6 edge-crossings — one full pole-to-pole zigzag circuit.
    The backward corpuscle is always 3 steps behind the forward (bilateral
    = half-circuit offset). [CC_M1, CC_M2, CC_M3]

  Tau (I52, 2 bilateral corpuscles):
    Period = 20 × (phi/3) × (L_J/c) — Hamiltonian circuit of all 20
    face-center nexuses. Bilateral corpuscles meet at step 10 (antipodal
    face center). [CC_T1, CC_T2, CC_T4]

Gluon and muon periods are commensurable (muon = 3 × gluon). The tau period
is INCOMMENSURABLE with both — phi is irrational, so the tau never returns to
the same relative gluon phase, providing aperiodic sampling of all 20 gluon
states. [CC_SYNC_1, CC_SYNC_2]

2a.1 Tau perturbation recovery (gluon-guided backtracking)

If the tau is deflected to a wrong face-center nexus (1-face perturbation
at cycle 5), it does NOT remain permanently out of phase. The gluon
amplitude maxima at each nexus [GH0b] guide the recovery:

  - From the wrong face, the tau continues its natural 72° deflections.
    GEOMETRIC RESULT [TNP1-TNP3]: both non-incoming neighbors are always
    at exactly 72° (dodecahedron vertex symmetry, TNP1).  A blind consistent
    direction would orbit a 5-face pentagon (dodecahedral ring, TNP2) --
    so the gluon amplitude at each nexus actively selects between the two
    equal-angle options.  With correct gluon selection, a full 20-face
    Hamiltonian circuit always exists from any wrong-face start (TNP3).
  - All 20 face centers are recovered in 18 extra steps (STAB_T1).
  - Recovery is efficient: 18 steps for 19 remaining faces (STAB_T2).
  - After recovery, normal Hamiltonian cycling resumes from cycle 6 onward
    with no residual error (STAB_T3).

The muon is robust by construction: any cyclic phase shift leaves the 6-vertex
circuit unchanged (STAB_M1, STAB_M2). The gluon period = 2 is preserved under
any phase swap (STAB_G1, STAB_G2).

CONCLUSION: All three corpuscle types return to full structural coverage after
a single-nexus perturbation. The tau continues its natural 72° deflections
from wherever it lands — no active rerouting required. The icosahedral geometry
guarantees full face-center coverage within 18 steps of the perturbation.


SECTION 3: LOSSLESS PROPAGATION — THE MAXWELL CRITERION [CC4-CC6]
------------------------------------------------------------------

For a force to permanently alter the cell's internal configuration, it would
need to scatter energy INTO the cell lattice. This is forbidden by the Maxwell
criticality of the Jobson cell (3V-E = 6, SY1):

3.1 Zero-energy soft modes

The icosahedral dynamical matrix has exactly 6 zero eigenvalues (SY11):
the T_1g and T_2g soft modes. Zero eigenvalue means zero frequency = zero
energy cost to propagate. T_1g and T_2g waves store NO energy in the lattice
bonds and experience no resistance. A force applied in a soft-mode direction
passes THROUGH the cell without depositing energy. [CC4]

3.2 No scattering channel at long wavelength

Maxwell criticality (3V-E = 6) places the lattice at the exact rigidity
transition. At this point, the soft modes (T_1g, T_2g) are DECOUPLED from the
hard-bond modes at long wavelength (k→0). There is no scattering channel from
the propagating soft modes into the constrained hard bonds. [CC5]
The gluon confinement underlying this decoupling is an algebraic identity:
chi(2G_g, C5) = −1 (phase flip at every vertex) and chi(2G_g, C3) = +1
(constructive at face centers) follow directly from n=5 edges/vertex and
Maxwell criticality alone — no dynamical assumption required. Gluons are
edge-confined; T_1g and T_2g are not; this is why the soft modes decouple.
[MN1-MN7]

3.3 Thermal energy cannot excite cells

The cell energy scale is T_cell = E_cell / k_B = 1.45×10^15 K. The hottest
known matter (QGP, ~2×10^12 K) is 0.0014 × T_cell. No physical environment
in the observable universe can thermally excite a Jobson cell. The cells stay
in their ground state; no absorption channel is thermally accessible. [CC6]

CONCLUSION: Below E_cell, T_1g and T_2g waves propagate without absorption.
Heat IS the disordered T_1g wave field. The medium is a cold, lossless substrate.
This is NOT a separate postulate — it follows from the geometry of the I_h cell.

(Scope condition: Pauli-locked states and Zone-1-excited configurations alter
the effective dynamical matrix. Dynamic rigidity and lossless propagation within
those states are Series 3 open items. See Section 6.)


SECTION 4: THE NYQUIST CONDITION — WHERE RIGIDITY ENDS [CC7-CC9]
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The rigid-body and lossless-propagation proofs are valid for ALL sub-E_cell
interactions. What happens AT E_cell?

The Nyquist condition (SY6, torsionverse_doc.py) states:

  E_Nyquist = hbar × (2π c / L_J) = 124.8 GeV = E_cell   [CC7]

This is the energy at which one full EM oscillation has a spatial period
exactly equal to L_J. At this energy, the wave can NO LONGER average over
multiple cells — it resolves the discrete lattice.

The TEMPORAL version of the same condition (SY20b):

  T_Nyquist = 2π hbar / E_cell = L_J / c = 3.31×10^-26 s   [CC8]

The photon period AT E_cell equals the single-corpuscle cell-edge transit time (L_J/c).
The cell can no longer respond before the next oscillation cycle — the coherence
condition breaks down.

Both the spatial (wavelength = L_J) and temporal (period = transit time)
Nyquist conditions give identical energies, confirming that E_cell is the
single UV cutoff of the torsionverse medium. [CC9]

THE THREE ENERGY REGIMES:

  E << E_cell:  Medium is continuous. T_1g/T_2g propagate as smooth waves.
                Cell is dynamically rigid. No absorption. EM and gravity
                described by T_1g and T_2g long-wavelength modes.

  E = E_cell:   Nyquist boundary. Wave resolves individual cells.
                Couples to A_g (isotropic bulk mode = Higgs). Energy goes
                INTO the cell structure, not through it. SSB / jamming.

  E > E_cell:   Wave sees cell discreteness. Can create new cells (cloning
                requires m_crit = 9.933 GeV; full cell creation requires
                E_cell). Above E_cell: medium becomes opaque to that wave.


SECTION 5: HIGGS AS UV CUTOFF — THE A_g CONNECTION [CC10]
----------------------------------------------------------

The A_g irreducible representation is the totally symmetric (isotropic) mode
of I_h. From jobson_cell_doc.py J13:

  T_1g × T_1g → A_g appears exactly once (unique scalar combination)

A_g is the Higgs field of the torsionverse (J7, J20, J21). Its mass is the
energy at which the I_h lattice reaches jamming (Maxwell criticality = SSB):
E_cell = 124.8 GeV. This is established in Series 1.

This paper adds the wave mechanics perspective: A_g is also the mode excited
AT the Nyquist boundary. When a wave with E = E_cell hits a cell, it cannot
continue as a T_1g propagating wave — it couples to the unique scalar A_g mode.
The energy goes into the isotropic bulk compression, which is jamming/SSB.

The Higgs mass is therefore the UV cutoff FROM TWO INDEPENDENT PERSPECTIVES:
  (1) Algebraic: A_g = jamming mode, mass = E_cell (J7, J20, Series 1)
  (2) Wave-mechanical: A_g = excitation at Nyquist boundary (this paper)

These are the same condition stated in two different languages. The Higgs
mass is not fine-tuned — it is the frequency at which the elastic icosahedral
medium transitions from a continuous propagating-wave substrate to a discrete
cell-interacting regime. [CC10]

NOTE ON THE HIERARCHY PROBLEM:
In standard QFT, the Higgs mass at ~125 GeV is unnaturally small compared to
the Planck scale (~10^19 GeV) — the hierarchy problem. In the torsionverse,
there is no hierarchy problem because the Higgs mass is DERIVED: it is L_J/c
in natural units, set by the icosahedral cell geometry (alpha, phi, r_p). The
"problem" does not arise because the Higgs is not a fundamental scalar with
arbitrary mass — it is the A_g mode of the I_h cell lattice.


SECTION 6: EXCEPTIONS — SCOPE OF THE RIGIDITY PROOF
-----------------------------------------------------

The dynamic rigidity (Section 2) and lossless propagation (Section 3) proofs
apply to the GROUND-STATE medium. Two configurations are outside this scope:

6.1 Pauli-locked state (Mode 3 of spinning pyramid, doc_series2_spinning_pyramid.txt)
    When cells are all in the same Hopf winding state, the effective dynamical
    matrix changes. The zero-eigenvalue argument (Section 3.1) may not hold —
    the locked cells have a different soft-mode structure. Whether a Pauli-locked
    region remains lossless and rigid is an open question. [Series 3 target]

6.2 Zone-1-excited configuration (strange quark G_u fluctuation, CA28b)
    A transient strange quark (G_u, m_strange ≈ m_p/2) entering Zone 1 locally
    modifies the cell's chi coupling. The soft-mode decoupling may temporarily
    fail in that region, enabling local energy absorption. This may be relevant
    to the proton's stability — the cell configuration around Zone 1 may
    self-adjust to remain non-absorbing in the ground state while allowing
    a transient absorbing path for excited states. [Series 3 target]

6.3 E+ vertex continuation rule
    The (1,2) Hopf winding of the electron is topologically unique (J20, V7-V8).
    The tau (I52) and muon (G32) bounce patterns are explicitly proven stable
    (CG9, CG17: 72° deflection at all steps). For the E+ mode, no script
    currently proves which of the 5 outgoing edges at each vertex the winding
    selects. The I_h symmetry implies uniqueness; the explicit geometric proof
    is a Series 3 target.


SECTION 7: CONCLUSIONS
------------------------

Three results establish the physical basis for treating Jobson cells as
rigid, lossless units in all sub-E_cell physics:

  1. DYNAMIC RIGIDITY [CC1-CC3]: bilateral tau sync time (5.39 × L_J/c = 1.79×10^-25 s)
     is shorter than the strong force timescale by 15.7× and EM by 2,150×.
     All face gluons synchronized; cell responds as a rigid unit.

  2. LOSSLESS PROPAGATION [CC4-CC6]: Maxwell criticality (3V-E=6) decouples
     soft modes from hard bonds. T_cell = 1.45×10^15 K is unreachable at any
     physical temperature. No absorption channel exists. Heat is disordered
     T_1g wave energy, not thermal excitation of the medium.

  3. HIGGS AS UV CUTOFF [CC7-CC10]: E_cell is the Nyquist boundary where both
     the spatial (λ = L_J) and temporal (T = L_J/c) conditions are satisfied.
     At this energy, the wave couples to A_g (Higgs) rather than propagating.
     The Higgs mass is derived, not fine-tuned; the hierarchy problem dissolves.

  4. CORPUSCLE CYCLE STABILITY [CC_G1-CC_T4, STAB_G1-STAB_T3]: all three
     corpuscle types recover from a single-nexus perturbation without residual
     error. Tau-lever Bode loop gain G_total = 3.75×10⁻⁴ (2,670× damping per
     circuit) confirms the synchronization in result 1 is actively stabilized,
     not a passive timescale bound. [GPS1-GPS6, BS1-BS5]
